Research

Gamma-ray burst science predictions for COSI

The Compton Spectrometer and Imager (COSI) is NASA's next astrophysics mission launching in summer 2027. As the Early Career Co-Lead for the COSI Gamma-Ray Burst Science Team, I lead predictions for the GRB science enabled by COSI, including estimating the expected detection rate of short-duration gamma-ray bursts and quantifying the constraints that its polarization measurements can place on gamma-ray burst physics. I also develop the COSI spectral and polarization analysis software and have created tools to simulate COSI transient observations.

Probing novel gamma-ray burst origins

I led analysis of the longest-duration gamma-ray burst ever observed, with gamma-ray emission lasting over seven hours. It cannot be explained by any known source of gamma-ray bursts, and we instead find that a plausible origin is a stellar-mass black hole merging with a star, consuming it from within, and ultimately destroying the star in an explosion. This work was featured in media outlets including a NASA press release, BBC Sky at Night, National Geographic, and Sky and Telescope.
