Eliza Hahn Neights

High-energy astrophysicist

I study the flashes of gamma rays produced by powerful jets that arise from events such as the explosive deaths of massive stars and collisions of neutron stars—the incredibly dense remains of exploded stars. I probe the origins and physical processes powering these gamma-ray bursts using telescopes in space, and I make predictions for the novel science enabled by next-generation instruments.

I am currently a Chateaubriand Fellow at the Institut de Recherche en Astrophysique et Planétologie in France while completing the final year of my PhD in physics at George Washington University. My PhD research has primarily been carried out at NASA Goddard Space Flight Center. I graduated from Penn State in 2021 with a BS in astronomy & astrophysics, BS in physics, and minor in mathematics.

Outside of research, I play harp, dance Brazilian zouk, boulder, and hike.
